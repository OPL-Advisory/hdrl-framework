## Privacy notice — HDRL self-assessment (public beta)

**Version [CONFIRM: version] · Effective [CONFIRM: date]**

### In short

Your assessment stays on your device. The levels you choose, your certainty, your notes, your evidence references and the report you generate are created and stored in your own browser. They are not sent to us, and we cannot retrieve them.

We collect three limited things: aggregate statistics about how the tool is used, which contain no identifier for you; an email address, role and organisation if you choose to unlock the report; and any feedback you choose to send us.

We will not email you about anything other than the beta unless you separately ask us to. Giving us your email address to unlock your report is not a marketing sign-up.

You can complain to us directly about how we handle your information, and to the Information Commissioner's Office if you are not satisfied.

### 2. What this notice covers

This notice covers the HDRL self-assessment public beta at `[CONFIRM: assessment route URL]`. It covers what happens when you use the tool, unlock a report, send feedback or ask us to contact you.

It does not cover the rest of the `hdrlframework.org` website, which has its own notice, and it does not cover what your own organisation does with an assessment or report you choose to share with it.

### 3. What stays on your device

The following never leaves your browser unless you deliberately export or share it:

- the assessment title, boundary, scope and time period you enter;
- every maturity level you select, and your certainty in each;
- your rationale, comments and improvement notes;
- your evidence references and notes; and
- the report you generate, in any format.

We do not receive this information and we cannot recover it. Our service is built so that it is not transmitted: the tool rejects any attempt to send assessment content to us rather than accepting it and removing it later.

Two things follow from this, and both matter to you. If you clear your browser data, use a different device or lose the device, your draft is gone and we cannot restore it — export it if you need a copy. And if you deliberately send us assessment content, for example in a share bundle or by pasting it into a support email, we will have received it, and this section no longer applies to that copy.

### 5. Analytics

*(expandable)*

We use Plausible Analytics to count how the tool is used. We do not use it to work out who you are.

Automatic page-view tracking is switched off on the assessment route. We send a short, fixed list of events — that the beta was started, that a section was completed, that the report was unlocked, that a download was requested, and that feedback was sent or skipped — together with coarse counts and time bands, the tool and framework version, and your screen size band.

We do not send your email address, your name, your organisation, any identifier for you or your session, any maturity level, any certainty, any assessment text, any evidence reference or any report content. The full list of what we send is published at `[CONFIRM: event allow-list URL]`.

We rely on the statistical purposes exception in the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, which allows this without asking for your consent provided we tell you clearly and give you a simple way to object. **You can turn beta analytics off at any time using the control in the tool.** It is free, it takes one action, and it stops immediately. Turning it off does not affect your report, your draft or any feedback you have chosen to send.

Note that "download requested" only tells us that you asked for a file. It does not tell us whether the file saved, or whether you opened it.

### 6. Email verification

*(expandable)*

To unlock the full report, we ask you to verify an email address. We send a six-digit code from **report@hdrlframework.org**. The code expires after 10 minutes, works once, and is never placed in a web address. The email contains no link, no assessment content and no marketing.

Verifying your address unlocks report generation in your browser. It does not send your assessment or your report to us.

We keep your verified email address, your role, your organisation and the dates on which you verified and last used the tool. Other profile information — your name, region, service type and approximate scale — is optional and you can leave it blank.

### 7. Feedback

*(expandable)*

Feedback is optional and you can skip it.

**Without contact details.** We store your rating, category and comment, along with limited context such as the tool version and which part of the tool you were in. We do not store your email address, your organisation, or any identifier linking the feedback to you or to the analytics.

We describe this as feedback *without contact details* rather than as anonymous feedback, and the distinction is deliberate. What you write could still identify you — an unusual situation described in a small field, or a phrase we would recognise — and our suppliers keep short-lived technical logs that we do not control. Please avoid putting confidential or personal information in the comment box.

**Contactable feedback.** If you separately choose this, we link your feedback to your participant record so we can reply. That is a distinct choice you make on the feedback form, not something that follows from unlocking the report.

### 8. Optional contact

*(expandable)*

You can withdraw either at any time by emailing privacy@hdrlframework.org or using the unsubscribe link. Withdrawal takes effect for the future and does not affect anything sent before. We keep a minimal record of your withdrawal so that we can honour it.

### 14. Security, and what we cannot promise

*(expandable)*

Information is encrypted in transit and at rest. Administrative access requires multi-factor authentication and is limited, logged and reviewed. Information you type into a free-text box may identify you even when we have not asked who you are. And information held only in your browser is exactly as secure as the device and browser you are using — if you share a computer, or your device is lost, we cannot protect your draft.

# Part D — checkbox labels and consent records

## D.1 Principles applied

Optional contact is never bundled with report access. The report gate collects what it needs and stops; the two optional choices sit visually below it, unticked, and are not a condition of anything. Contactable feedback is a third, separate choice made on the feedback form. No checkbox is pre-ticked, no double negatives are used, and no choice is expressed as an opt-out.

## D.2 Labels

**At the report gate — required fields, no consent checkbox**

> Email address (required) · Role (required) · Organisation `[CONFIRM: required or optional]`
>
> Name, region, service type and approximate scale are optional.
>
> *We use these to verify your address, unlock your report and administer the beta. We will not email you about anything else unless you ask us to below.*

**Below the gate, visually separated, both unticked**

> **[ ] Research contact.** OPL Advisory may email me about voluntary HDRL research or testing.
>
> **[ ] Updates.** OPL Advisory may email me occasional HDRL updates.
>
> *Both are optional and separate. You will get your report either way. You can withdraw either at any time.*

**On the feedback form, unticked**

> **[ ] Reply to me.** Link this feedback to my participant record so OPL Advisory can contact me about it.

**Where results are shared**

Result sharing is an action, not a checkbox. The participant selects categories, sees what is included, and confirms. Record the bundle scope and the wording version; do not record the contents.

## D.3 Consent records

The existing `beta_contact_preferences` schema is well designed for this and needs no change. Record, for each choice:

| Field | Content |
|:--|:--|
| Participant | Reference to the participant record |
| Purpose | `research_contact`, `newsletter` or `contactable_feedback` |
| Wording version | The exact version of the label shown, matched to the published notice version |
| Affirmative action | That the box was ticked, with the interface context |
| Date and time | When the choice was made |
| Withdrawn date | When it was withdrawn, left empty until then |

Two rules that make these records defensible. Never write a preference record by inference — an unticked box produces no record at all, not a record of refusal. And keep the withdrawal record after the underlying record is deleted, because it is the only thing that lets you honour the withdrawal afterwards.

## D.4 What must not appear

No combined label of the form "I agree to the privacy notice and to receiving updates". No consent to the privacy notice at all — a notice is information, not something to agree to. No pre-ticked boxes. No wording that implies the report depends on an optional choice. No "unsubscribe at any time" used as a reason to make the choice look inconsequential.
