# Product refresh: learning from SmarterX

**Reviewed:** 23 October 2026. Product research and design recommendations; not independent validation of either instrument.

## Recommended direction

Finish the existing HDRL beta around the 64-indicator snapshot, improve the usefulness of its report, then add voluntary local team comparison. Evaluate an evidence assistant in a separate, bounded experiment. The comparison does not justify a platform rewrite, a replacement scoring model or immediate confidential-document ingestion.

The important parallel is turning a framework into something people can apply, understand and act on. The strongest opportunity is the connection from a response to a useful next question or action. HDRL's distinguishing value remains the separation of current understanding, evidence and operating capacity within an explicit assessment boundary.

The [current build brief](current-build-brief.md) gives the ordered work and acceptance criteria. The original July [research](research-and-design.md) and August [privacy review](operations-and-roadmap.md#current-activation-state) remain relevant inputs.

## What was examined

- The live SmarterX public landing and entry descriptions, its current FAQ and its founder's published development account. No account was created and no questionnaire submitted. Public HTML inspection is not a visual, keyboard, authenticated-team or security test.
- The live HDRL website/catalogue and the current branch, product requirements, application, exports, privacy pack and deployment workflow.
- Both supplied Grok/Gemini analyses, retained as A and B under `reference/self-assessment-review-2026-10-23/`; their pasted texts do not identify authors individually.
- A separate agent review conducted without reading A or B. Its conclusions are retained alongside them. Agreement between models is a useful cross-check, not validation.

## Comparator facts and their limits

The [live SmarterX application](https://score.smarterx.ai/) identifies itself as public beta. It advertises 67 Business statements across eight pillars in 12–15 minutes, and 37 Personal statements across five pillars in 8–10 minutes. It describes autosave, contextual explanations, a visual profile and links from statements to action ladders. These are product descriptions and advertised times, not observed completion distributions.

The [current FAQ](https://smarterx.ai/ai-score-faq) adds important qualifications: “Not Sure” contributes zero; Business registration precedes answering; a team total can begin with one respondent; role/function segments need five. Administrators can see individual Business responses. Independent answers are encouraged, but an enforced blind round and facilitated consensus are not established. Fixed grades are not peer percentiles. Detailed weighting, measurement validity and conversion/retention performance were not independently established.

The [September development account](https://smarterx.ai/smarterxblog/smarterx-ai-score) describes an estimated 300–400 founder hours and numerous AI prototypes before a development team took over production work. Its claimed savings and delivery estimates are self-reported. The [20 October launch discussion](https://podcast.smarterx.ai/shownotes/247) describes a soft public beta and a further November rollout. Earlier waitlist copy should not be treated as current launch status.

**Implication:** SmarterX demonstrates a coherent product proposition and a founder-to-production workflow. It does not supply evidence that HDRL can match its completion time, that its scoring is appropriate for HDRL, or that one operator can support the same hosted feature set.

The model recommendations to shorten the framework or introduce pass/fail should therefore be treated as alternatives to test, not accepted requirements. Both supplied analyses are strongest when they argue for contextual help, visible disagreement, provenance and useful next actions.

## An offer that can be run with little manual support

The public offer should answer: **“What do we currently think, how sure are we, and what should we investigate next?”** The first visit should show a fictional example output, explain the boundary, and lead into one domain at a time. Evidence work can be added later without discarding the initial responses.

The proposed next-step pack should be original HDRL guidance, independently versioned from the canonical catalogue. Each item should contain a trigger, indicator references, the relevant evidence expectation, a question/action, limitations and a guidance version. An unknown response triggers a question about who can help; missing evidence triggers an evidence-gathering action; a capability constraint triggers a planning discussion. These are not interchangeable.

Users should choose their next three priorities, nominate an owner locally and add a review date. Any displayed shortlist should explain its ordering and allow changes. The lowest maturity level must not automatically become the highest investment priority, and L5 must not become a universal target.

Most support should be handled through examples, a concise FAQ, accessible help and a structured feedback form that makes contact optional. The operator should receive a compact exception/feedback queue. Do not create automated follow-ups, new integrations or recurring jobs simply to implement this recommendation; those need an actual operating requirement and, for messages, the user's instruction.

### Team use before hosted workspaces

Have participants complete the same scoped snapshot independently, then explicitly share a limited export with their own facilitator through an approved organisational route. The facilitator imports compatible files locally and sees per-indicator distributions, respondent counts, certainty and applicability differences. Optional role labels should be broad; the tool should avoid identifying small groups in shared outputs.

The comparison must match framework/catalogue/guidance versions and catalogue hash, assessment stage, boundary and time period. Reject incompatible formats/versions and require explicit scope reconciliation where necessary. Preserve original answers and record any group judgement, rationale and actions separately.

This local workflow cannot authenticate respondents, enforce blind submissions, revoke a copied file or provide a tamper-resistant audit. Those are reasons to consider hosted teams later, not capabilities to claim now. Begin with one shared boundary and a few independent responses; test whether the result is useful before building invitations and tenant administration.

### Reassessment

Retakes need a frozen earlier export and an explicit difference record for scope, method, respondents, catalogue and evidence dates. A changed response may mean better knowledge or a different interpretation rather than improved capability. No automated trend comparison should cross incompatible versions or boundaries.

## Evidence-assistant experiment

Begin with a retrieval/extraction assistant that produces a **candidate evidence register**, not a maturity result. Use supplied public documents or synthetic excerpts with controlled scope; respect publication and reuse permissions. Detailed three-nation assessments, submitted evidence and Right-of-Reply material are not an authorised evaluation corpus merely because formative HDRL work used them.

Each candidate should identify the catalogue version/hash, indicator, document URL/reference and hash, title/date, retrieval date, exact page/section or text location, a short supporting extract, the claim supported, limitations, contrary evidence and a follow-up question. Record model, prompt and run versions. A record starts as unreviewed and can be accepted, amended or rejected without overwriting the original proposal. Confidence describes the extraction/mapping task; it does not certify the service.

Explicitly distinguish “not located in the reviewed sources”, “source unavailable”, “outside the reviewed scope”, “contradictory” and “requires internal evidence”. None should assign L1 or negate an assessor's judgement. Keep the assistant's suggestions separate from independent team responses to reduce anchoring.

Use at least 24 deliberately varied cases: strong, partial, outdated, aspirational, irrelevant, wrong-service and contradictory evidence; missing sources; adjacent-level ambiguity; and malicious instructions in documents. Reserve an unseen subset before prompt iteration. Use a human reference for relevance/citation/limitations, with contested cases marked unresolved. Neither historical HDRL judgements nor a second model are automatic ground truth.

Proposed acceptance checks for this research experiment are product choices, not standards:

- every proposal identifies an allowed indicator and an actual source location;
- every source/quotation used in the small release set is manually checked; any fabricated citation or unsupported assurance claim stops promotion;
- no proposal changes the user's judgement or treats missing public evidence as capability absent;
- failures, corrections, abstentions, cost and run-to-run differences are retained;
- net reviewer time, including checking and correction, improves against manual mapping; and
- the model has no email, supplier-console, database-write or unrestricted network tools.

The [NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/prompt-injection-is-not-sql-injection) recommends deterministic limits around what models can do because prompt injection remains a residual risk. [NIST's GenAI profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) provides a voluntary framework for designing and evaluating the experiment. A successful pilot would justify a later integration decision; it would not establish accreditation or independent assurance.

## What to measure first

Measure whether intended users can complete a domain, resume, understand the report and choose useful next actions without the developer present. Retain 5–10 minutes for optional orientation and 30–60 minutes for the full snapshot as unvalidated planning estimates. Observe active time separately from elapsed time and domain coverage. Treat 128 selections as a minimum interaction count, not a timing study.

Track starts, completion/partial completion, requested exports and feedback submitted/skipped through the approved event boundary. Measure support minutes and unresolved issues as operating costs. For the evidence experiment, the primary benefit is **net human review time saved at an acceptable error level**, not the number of model outputs. Small, self-selected beta cohorts cannot support population estimates or maturity benchmarking.
