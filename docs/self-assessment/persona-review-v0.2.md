# Rapid-question persona review (v0.2)

This review accompanies—not replaces—participant testing. It tests whether the eight rapid prompts are recognisable to people operating a health-data service and understandable to people outside the delivery team. It does not validate that eight questions predict a full 64-indicator assessment.

## Persona 1: service operator and assessment facilitator

This persona needs wording that maps to observable operations, keeps capability separate from capacity and gives a mixed team enough specificity to discuss the same boundary.

| Domain | Review finding | Revision made |
|---|---|---|
| A | “Available” could include data that exist but are not routinely linkable or refreshed. | Added consistency, linkage, currency and the stated assessment boundary. |
| B | The first wording omitted pooled and federated interoperability. | Added quality assessment, common models, interoperability and combined analysis. |
| C | Governance sounded document-led. | Reframed around routes for approving and enabling access in practice. |
| D | “Real research” was ambiguous and could devalue internal research. | Replaced it with external researchers, cross-organisation collaboration and reproducible delivery. |
| E | “How visibly are…” was grammatically awkward. | Reframed around how clearly the service demonstrates transparency, public benefit and involvement. |
| F | Current funding alone does not show resilience. | Added resilience, operating model and evidence of value. |
| G | Skills without funded headroom can overstate readiness. | Kept workforce capacity explicit and tied it to dependable delivery. |
| H | A pilot environment can appear mature without routine load. | Added “under realistic demand”. |

## Persona 2: external researcher and public contributor

This persona needs plain language, transparent limits and prompts that do not assume access to internal jargon or documents.

| Domain | Review finding | Revision made |
|---|---|---|
| A | “Services can work together” needed an operational anchor. | The supporting prompt now distinguishes routine practice from a pilot. |
| B | “Semantics” is framework language, not a useful first question. | The question uses “understood, quality-assessed and combined”; technical examples remain expandable. |
| C | “Proportionate” can hide whose experience is being considered. | The prompt points to understandable, dependable routes and practical decision times. |
| D | Project counts alone do not describe the researcher experience. | Support and reproducibility remain explicit. |
| E | Publication is not the same as useful transparency or influence. | The guidance includes accessible reporting, lay governance and feedback loops. |
| F | “Sustainable” can sound like a simple yes/no claim. | “Resilient” invites consideration of future funding and operating risk. |
| G | Culture is otherwise abstract. | The prompt links roles, skills, headroom and culture to delivery. |
| H | Certification can be mistaken for the whole answer. | The prompt balances security with usability, resilience and scale. |

## Resulting design choices

- Keep one prompt per domain for the rapid pass and label the output as a preliminary learning profile.
- Use five named impression bands without numeric scores.
- Record certainty as **high, medium or low**, in that order; do not use a 1–10 scale because it would imply unsupported precision.
- Separate “Not known” and “Not assessed” visually and semantically from the five impression bands.
- Test the revised wording with service operators, researchers, public contributors and mixed assessment groups before treating it as stable.

The versioned wording is in `hdrl-assessment-content-v0.2.0.json`. Canonical indicator wording remains in the machine-readable HDRL catalogue and is not changed by this review.
